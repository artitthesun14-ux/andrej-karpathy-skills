# Presentation Principles

Read when setting art direction, layout, charts, navigation, or responsive behavior for a deck.

## Art direction checklist

- Mood in three words (for example: calm, precise, warm).
- Type: one display face and one text face, with a size scale of at most 5 steps. Headline carries the message as a sentence.
- Color: one neutral base, one accent, one signal color for emphasis or warning. Check contrast.
- Imagery: one consistent treatment (photo, illustration, or abstract) and crop rules.
- Chart style: shared axis, label, and color rules across the deck.

## Slide roles

Mix roles across the deck: opener, section break, claim with one visual, evidence (chart), comparison, process, quote or proof, summary, call to action. A deck of only "title plus bullets" fails.

## Composition

- Align to a grid with consistent margins. Break the grid on purpose, for emphasis.
- Use scale contrast: one dominant element, one supporting, the rest quiet.
- Give whitespace to the key idea. Cut anything not supporting the slide's single message.

## Charts for newcomers

- Title states the finding, not the topic.
- Label directly on the chart. Avoid legends when 3 or fewer series.
- Start bar axes at zero. Show units and source.
- Highlight one series or point, mute the rest.

## Navigation and responsive

- Arrow keys, click, swipe. Progress indicator. Escape or overview for orientation.
- Scale type with viewport. Below tablet width, stack layouts and keep one idea per screen.
- Scripts without spaces between words (Thai, Chinese, Japanese) can break mid-word in headlines. Check breaks in the render and keep words together with `white-space: nowrap` spans or `<wbr>` hints.

## Investor audience (beginners)

Explain terms once in plain words. Use one running example. Separate facts from projections and label projections as such.
